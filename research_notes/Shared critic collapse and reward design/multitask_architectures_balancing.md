## 1. Supervised multi-task architectures (MMoE, PLE, cross-stitch, MTAN, soft sharing, adapters/FiLM, hypernetworks): mechanism, cost, benefit over hard sharing, use in critics

### Takeaway
All the partial-sharing designs from supervised multi-task learning (MTL) do the same thing. They give each task some parameters that only that task's loss trains (towers, task-specific experts, per-task columns, adapters), and a learned mechanism (gates, linear mixing, attention, affine modulation) decides how much shared signal each task uses. I found no published use of MMoE, PLE, cross-stitch, MTAN, adapters/LoRA or FiLM-with-heads as an online TD-trained critic with one head per objective. The nearest work is: MoE layers in single-task value networks (Obando-Ceron et al. 2024); a hypernetwork critic for TD3/SAC that improves the action-gradient dQ/da (Keynan, Sarafian & Kraus 2021); and a suggestion, with no experiment, to use MMoE for several critics trained offline (Cai et al. 2022). Of these designs, only PLE-style task-specific experts and per-task columns (cross-stitch) structurally keep one task's gradient off the other tasks' parameters. That property is the one H1/H4 say we need.

### Cited Findings
- **MMoE** (Ma, Zhao, Yi, Chen, Hong, Chi; KDD 2018). The expert sub-networks are shared across all tasks, and each task has its own gating network. The authors motivate it by noting that the prediction quality of common multi-task models "is often sensitive to the relationships between tasks". On synthetic data with controlled task relatedness, the approach "performs better than baseline methods when the tasks are less related". It also shows "an additional trainability benefit" that depends on the randomness in the training data and in model initialisation. [A] — [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/modeling-task-relationships-in-multi-task-learning-with-multi-gate-mixture-of-experts/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3219819.3220007)
- **PLE** (Tang, Liu, Zhao, Gong; RecSys 2020). It targets the "seesaw phenomenon": improving one task lowers others when tasks are "complexly and competitively correlated". PLE separates shared and task-specific components explicitly (task-specific experts plus shared experts) and uses progressive routing through stacked gated layers. It outperforms state-of-the-art MTL models on a Tencent video dataset of about 1 billion samples, gains online, and is deployed. [A, paraphrased abstract] — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1145/3383313.3412236); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3383313.3412236)
- **Cross-stitch networks** (Misra, Shrivastava, Gupta, Hebert; CVPR 2016). Each task has its own network. At chosen layers a learned linear combination mixes the activation maps of the task columns, and the authors "maintain one cross-stitch unit per 'channel'". Their motivating observation: "In practice, different amount of sharing tends to work best for different tasks". Cross-stitch did better "than the best Split architecture network found using brute force search", and the mixing "helps the 'data-starved' tasks". [S] — [arXiv 1604.03539](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03539)
- **MTAN** (Liu, Johns, Davison; CVPR 2019). A shared network plus task-specific soft-attention modules. On CityScapes it "performs similarly to our baseline Dense ... whilst only having less than half the number of parameters". It "shows greater robustness to the choice of weighting scheme in the loss function compared to baselines". The paper also introduces Dynamic Weight Average (DWA, Section 3 below). [S] — [arXiv 1803.10704](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10704)
- **Soft parameter sharing.** Ruder's overview names hard and soft parameter sharing as the two most common deep-MTL methods. Soft sharing (each task keeps its own model, and a penalty keeps the parameters close) does not reduce the parameter count. [A; the body's definition was not re-read here] — [Ruder 2017, arXiv 1706.05098](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05098)
- **Adapters.** Residual adapter modules let one network be "steered on the fly to diverse visual domains" with "a high degree of parameter sharing while maintaining or even improving the accuracy of domain-specific representations" (Rebuffi, Bilen, Vedaldi; NeurIPS 2017) [A] — [arXiv 1705.08045](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08045). Adapter modules "add only a few trainable parameters per task" on a frozen shared network (Houlsby et al.; ICML 2019) [A] — [arXiv 1902.00751](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)
- **FiLM** (Perez, Strub, de Vries, Dumoulin, Courville; AAAI 2018): "a simple, feature-wise affine transformation based on conditioning information". [A] — [arXiv 1709.07871](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07871)
- **Hypernetworks** (Ha, Dai, Le; ICLR 2017): one network generates the weights of another. [A] — [arXiv 1609.09106](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09106)
- **Hypernetwork critic in TD3/SAC** (Keynan, Sarafian, Kraus; ICML 2021). Off-policy actor-critics "utilize only the parametric neural gradient of the critic with respect to the action input". Concatenating state and action "may lead to poor gradient estimation in actor-critic algorithms". A hypernetwork critic with the state as the meta-variable (the state generates the weights of a network that takes the action) "enjoys better gradient accuracy which translates into a faster learning rate". The authors report consistent gains with TD3 and SAC on locomotion. This is the only architecture paper I found that targets dQ/da quality directly (H2). It is single-task. [S] — [arXiv 2106.06842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06842)
- **Deciding what to share.** Branched multi-task networks are built automatically from task affinities under a parameter budget, so that "shallow layers are task-agnostic, whereas deeper ones gradually grow more task-specific" (Vandenhende et al.; BMVC 2020) [A] — [arXiv 1904.02920](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02920). Recon (Shi et al.; ICLR 2023) reports that "'gradient surgery' cannot effectively reduce the occurrence of conflicting gradients" and that gradient manipulation methods "can only slightly reduce the occurrence of conflicting gradients ... and in some other cases they even increase it". Recon computes per-layer conflict scores during training, turns the highest-conflict shared layers into task-specific layers, and retrains, "with only a slight increase in model parameters (less than 1% in some cases)". [S] — [arXiv 2302.11289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11289). RotoGrad traces negative transfer to "disparities in gradient magnitudes and directions across tasks" and homogenises both (Javaloy & Valera; ICLR 2022) [A] — [arXiv 2103.02631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02631)
- **MoE inside value networks (single-task).** Putting Soft MoE modules "into value-based networks results in more parameter-scalable models". The MoE replaces the penultimate layer of DQN-family networks (Obando-Ceron, Sokar, Willi, Lyle, Farebrother, Foerster, Dziugaite, Precup, Castro; ICML 2024, venue from memory). [S] — [arXiv 2402.08609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08609)
- **MMoE for several critics (offline suggestion only).** When the critics for different responses share one offline dataset, multi-critic learning "can be formulated into a multi-task learning problem". The shared-bottom model "may bring in optimization conflict from different tasks for the bottom parameters", and "One remedy is ... (MMOE)". I saw no experiment on this in the appendix. [S, appendix D] — [Cai et al. 2022, arXiv 2205.13248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13248)
- **Expert ensemble on the actor side for multi-objective RL** (Thakar & Tumer; ALA workshop 2026). A monolithic preference-conditioned network is "susceptible to destructive gradient interference". Replacing each agent's head with an ensemble of expert networks plus a preference-conditioned router gave "a 22% improvement in hypervolume compared to a parameter-matched baseline". The experiments are preliminary (MAMuJoCo) and the change is to the actor, not the critic. [A] — [E2M2 PDF](https://alaworkshop2026.github.io/papers/ALA2026_paper_61.pdf)
- **Naive MoE in multi-task RL can be weak.** Soft Modularization's "Mix-Expert" baseline ("four experts with the same architecture as MT-SAC, and a learned gating network") reached 42.8% on MT10-Fixed, against 85.0% for multi-head SAC [S] — [Yang et al. 2020, arXiv 2003.13661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13661). MOORE's "MOE" baseline, a mixture of experts "without enforcing diversity across experts", lost to the orthogonal version [S] — [Hendawy et al. 2024, arXiv 2311.11385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11385)

### Inferences
- **Parameter cost in our setting** (my count, one twin):

| Towers: shared L1, private L2 + head (tested) | 19,204 | 2.86x | 0.74x |
| Split 4 x 64-64 (tested) | 26,116 | 3.88x | 1.00x |
| MMoE, 4 shared experts 32-32, gate + tower per group | 9,540 | 1.42x | 0.37x |
| MMoE, 6 experts | 14,244 | 2.12x | 0.55x |
| CGC/PLE (1 level): 2 shared + 1 private expert per group (32-32), gate per group | 13,812 | 2.05x | 0.53x |
| Cross-stitch, 4 columns 32-32, scalar alpha per column pair per layer | 8,996 | 1.34x | 0.34x |
| Cross-stitch, 4 columns 64-64 | 26,148 | 3.89x | 1.00x |
| Soft sharing (4 x 64-64 plus a distance penalty) | 26,116 | 3.88x | 1.00x |
| LoRA rank 4 / rank 8 per group on both hidden layers | 10,356 / 13,988 | 1.54x / 2.08x | 0.40x / 0.54x |
| FiLM per group on both hidden layers, scalar output, 4 passes | 7,553 | 1.12x | 0.29x |
| One-hot group id as input, scalar output, 4 passes | 6,785 | 1.01x | 0.26x |

  A hypernetwork that outputs per-group weights from a one-hot group id is just a lookup table (4 separate networks), unless it is low-rank. A Keynan-style state-conditioned hypernetwork that generates even small per-group action-pathway weights needs an output layer of several hundred units, so it will most likely exceed the split critic. I did not size this precisely.
- **The structural question is whether the safety group's gradient can reach a group's parameters.** In MMoE every expert is shared, so the 100-3000x safety gradient trains every expert, and the per-group gates can only choose among safety-shaped experts. I therefore expect MMoE to collapse like the shared trunk. PLE/CGC adds experts that only their own group's loss trains. That makes it the only standard supervised design that structurally protects the small-scale groups (H4) while still sharing. Cross-stitch columns initialised to the identity start as a narrow split critic, but any learned mixing alpha lets the safety gradient flow into the other columns in proportion to alpha. FiLM, LoRA and adapters keep a fully shared base that the safety loss dominates, with small private deltas on top.
- **What a shared trunk does to action-slopes (H2).** With a shared trunk and linear heads, each group's action-slope field is dQ_k/da = sum_j w_kj dphi_j/da. Every group's slope is therefore a combination of the same 64 feature slopes. If the features are shaped by safety, the slope a group like efficiency needs (for example, the gradient of an action-magnitude cost) may lie outside their span, even when the values fit well. Private per-group parameters (PLE experts, towers, columns) give each group its own slope basis. This is my argument, not a cited result. It is consistent with the prompt's observation that "value accuracy on replay data does not predict control quality" ([prompt §4](/Users/khushpatel/code/CrazyFlie-2.1/l2f/docs/shared_critic_research_prompt.md)).
- **Our towers result in branched-MTL terms.** The towers design is a branched network that branches after layer 1. Recon's logic says to branch where the measured conflict is largest. Nobody has measured whether that is layer 1 or layer 2 in our critic.

### Gaps
- No paper found that applies PLE, cross-stitch, MTAN, adapters/LoRA or FiLM (with per-objective heads) to an online TD critic. MMoE for critics appears only as an offline suggestion with no reported result (Cai et al.).
- MMoE and PLE quantitative results were not read (abstracts only).
- "ScaleMoE" (listed as an ICML 2026 spotlight; a search snippet says it places MoE in both the actor and the critic for continuous control, with output-level and feature-level gating) could not be verified. The ICML page returned 404 and I found no arXiv match by title.
- RMTL (WWW 2023, [arXiv 2302.03328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03328)) reportedly uses a separate critic per task. I did not read it.

## 2. Multi-task RL architectures (Soft Modularization, CARE, MOORE, PaCo, D'Eramo et al., Meta-World SAC baselines): critic structure and findings about sharing in the critic

### Takeaway
In multi-task RL on Meta-World, the critic almost always gets the same architecture as the actor (with its own weights). The leading methods all give the critic task-specific parameters or a diversity constraint (Soft-Mod routing, CARE encoder mixing, PaCo parameter composition, MOORE orthogonal experts). PaCo's ablation shows the critic's structure matters: 85.4% with composition in actor and critic, against 73.3% in the actor only. Papers report three sources of harm in shared critics: tasks whose losses explode and dominate the shared parameters (PaCo, citing CARE's "stop-relaunch"); reward-scale disparity (PopArt, BRC, Kurin et al.); and negative interference in general. MultiCriticAL (separate critics per behaviour style) is the closest setting to ours. **Caveat:** in all of these the "tasks" are separate MDPs or reward functions, each with its own data and its own policy behaviour. None is a decomposed critic, in which the groups share transitions and the actor follows the real-unit sum.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta-World baselines.** MT-SAC uses "a one-hot task ID with the state as inputs"; MT-MH-SAC is "Built upon MT-SAC with independent heads for tasks". In Soft-Mod's own re-runs: MT10-Fixed MT-SAC 44.0%, MT-MH-SAC 85.0%; MT50-Fixed 31.4% and 35.5%. [S] — [Yang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13661). Benchmark: [Yu et al., Meta-World, CoRL 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10897) [S]
- **Soft Modularization** (Yang, Xu, Wu, Wang; NeurIPS 2020). "we adopt similar architectures with soft modularization for Q-function as well. The weights for both the base policy network and the routing network are not shared or reused in the Q-function." Per-task loss weights w_i = exp(-alpha_i)/sum_j exp(-alpha_j), built from the per-task SAC temperatures, multiply both the policy and the Q-function objectives. Results: MT10-Fixed 87.0% (vs MT-MH-SAC 85.0%); MT50-Fixed 59.5-60.0% (vs 35.5%). Capacity alone did not close the gap: "MT-MH-SAC-5-Wide" with 4.2x the parameters reached 50.3% on MT50-Fixed, against 60.0% for Soft-Mod. [S] — [arXiv 2003.13661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13661)
- **CARE** (Sodhani, Zhang, Pineau; ICML 2021). A mixture of k encoders, with attention weights computed from a task-metadata context, gives the state encoding. The actor and critic are "three fully connected layers with 400 units". MT10 success: MT-SAC 0.49, multi-headed SAC 0.61, PCGrad 0.72, Soft-Mod 0.73, SAC+FiLM 0.75 (FiLM layers "condition the encoder on the context"), CARE 0.84, one SAC per task 0.90. [S] — [arXiv 2102.06177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06177)
- **PaCo** (Sun, Zhang, Xu, Tomizuka; NeurIPS 2022). Each task's parameter vector, covering "policy and critic networks", is theta_tau = Phi w_tau: K shared parameter vectors plus a per-task composition vector. On interference: "some task losses may explode during training on Meta-World ..., which will contribute a significant portion in updating the shared parameters because of their dominance. As a consequence, this will significantly impact the training of the other tasks through the shared parameters." The fix is "loss maskout" (drop any task loss above a threshold) plus a w-reset. MT10-rand: MT-SAC 62.9, multi-head SAC 62.0, SAC+FiLM 58.3, PCGrad 61.7, Soft-Module 63.0, CARE 76.0, PaCo 85.4. Ablation: K=3/5/8 gives 74.4/85.4/81.0. Composition applied to actor and critic ("AC-shared") gives 85.4, actor only 73.3, output layer only 64.0. [S] — [arXiv 2210.11653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11653)
- **MOORE** (Hendawy, Peters, D'Eramo; ICLR 2024). Expert representations are orthonormalised with Gram-Schmidt and mixed with task-specific weights. "we employ this architecture for both the actor and the critic". The aim is to "avoid collapsing to similar representations". MT10-rand at 20M steps: 88.7 (k=4 experts) vs PaCo 85.4. MT50-rand: 72.9 (k=6) vs PaCo 57.3, CARE 50.8, MTSAC 49.3, SAC+FiLM 36.5. [S] — [arXiv 2311.11385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11385)
- **D'Eramo, Tateo, Bonarini, Restelli, Peters** (ICLR 2020). Task-specific input layers w_t, a shared representation h, and task-specific outputs f_t. For multi-task DDPG, "both the actor and critic networks should be built following our proposed structure". In the critic, "the actions a(t) are given as input to h" and "each f_t has a single linear unit Q_t". Their approximate value/policy iteration bounds show "the cost of learning the shared representation at each AVI iteration is mitigated by using multiple tasks". MDDPG beat DDPG on the pendulum set, beat it on Hopper, and matched it on Walker and Half-Cheetah. [S] — [arXiv 2401.09561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09561)
- **PopArt multi-task** (Hessel, Soyer, Espeholt, Czarnecki, Schmitt, van Hasselt; AAAI 2019). The value function has "N outputs, one for each task" on a shared torso, and "For each rollout, only the ith head in the value net is updated". Both the value targets and the policy-gradient advantages are normalised per task (their eqs. 11-12). The last layer is rescaled to "preserve unnormalised outputs" when the statistics change (eq. 13). Motivation: some tasks "appear more salient to the learning process, for instance because of the density or magnitude of the in-task rewards". A "MultiHead-IMPALA" with task-specific value heads but no PopArt "performs slightly worse than the original IMPALA". The unclipped-reward baseline's median score fell to "close to 0%", while PopArt-IMPALA scored about the same with or without clipping. [S] — [arXiv 1809.04474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04474)
- **BRC** (Nauman, Cygan, Sferrazza, Kumar, Abbeel; NeurIPS 2025). Multi-task TD learning is brittle because "sparse rewards and gradient conflicts make optimization of temporal difference brittle"; "tasks with higher rewards produce higher TD errors and stronger gradient signals". "Using task embeddings is preferable to separate heads design": task embeddings won at every scale tested (about 4M-256M parameters), and "the separate heads design performs better than single task oracle only past certain model scale". "the rate of gradient conflicts decreases as the number of parameters increases ..., showing that complex interventions such as gradient surgery might not be required after a certain model scale." [S] — [arXiv 2505.23150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23150)
- **MultiCriticAL** (Mysore, Cheng, Zhao, Saenko, Wu; ICLR 2022). "Using a single value function (critic) shared over multiple tasks" can cause negative interference between tasks. MultiCriticAL keeps a separate critic for each task and trains one multi-task actor. Reported gains are up to 56% over single-critic baselines, and it learns some styles where single-critic methods fail. The setting is multi-style learning (distinct behaviour styles for one agent). [A] — [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6568)
- **Kurin et al.** (NeurIPS 2022). In their Meta-World runs, "The actor is shared across all tasks as well as the critic" (three layers of 400). Rewards are normalised per task, r'_i = r_i / sigma_i, at sampling time. [S] — [arXiv 2201.04122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04122)
- **Critic-loss blow-ups are handled ad hoc in the standard codebase.** The mtrl-based SAC agents shipped with FAMO raise an error and stop training when "critic_loss ... is too high" (above 1e8). [C] — [FAMO repo, mtrl_files/uw.py and famo.py](https://github.com/Cranial-XIX/FAMO). PaCo calls CARE's practice of discarding such runs "stop-relaunch". [S] — [arXiv 2210.11653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11653)
- **RL-specific interference background.** "TD easily leads to low-interference, under-generalizing parameters"; the authors trace this to "the interplay between the dynamics of interference and bootstrapping" (Bengio, Pineau, Precup; ICML 2020, venue from memory) [A] — [arXiv 2003.06350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06350). "Ray interference" is a coupling between learning and data generation that can "constrain the agent to learn one thing at a time even when learning in parallel is better" (Schaul, Borsa, Modayil, Pascanu; 2019) [A] — [arXiv 1904.11455](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.11455)

### Inferences
- The multi-task RL evidence supports putting task-specific parameters in the critic, not only in the actor: PaCo's AC-shared vs actor-only ablation (+12 points), Soft-Mod's routed Q-function, and MOORE's experts in the critic.
- Two results parallel ours. (1) Hessel et al.'s "MultiHead-IMPALA" (heads without scale handling) was slightly worse than the scalar-value IMPALA, as our unscaled 4-head critic is worse than variant E. (2) BRC's "separate heads only beat single-task past a certain scale" parallels our shared 4-head critic losing to 4 separate networks. BRC's curves start at about 4M parameters; ours is about 7k.
- On capacity: Soft-Mod's 4.2x-wider multi-head baseline and our 142-unit shared critic (no improvement) both say that capacity alone does not remove interference at the scales tested. BRC suggests it can at millions of parameters, which is irrelevant for a 64-unit critic.
- The evidence conflicts on task embeddings versus heads. In Soft-Mod's re-runs one-hot conditioning was far worse than heads (44% vs 85%, MT10-Fixed). In BRC, task embeddings beat heads (much larger nets, cross-entropy loss, normalised residual blocks). Neither is directly transferable to 4 reward components on the same transitions.
- PaCo's quote describes our H4 mechanism almost word for word: one task's oversized loss dominates the shared parameters and damages the others. Their fix (masking the exploding loss) is not available to us, because the large safety loss is the signal the actor needs (H1). That points to giving safety its own parameters instead (Section 5).
- In multi-task RL each task's policy behaviour is separate, so balancing tasks is the goal. In our decomposed critic the actor must follow the real-unit sum. Rebalancing the critic's shared features therefore changes what the actor's most important slope (safety) is built from, without changing the actor's objective. The multi-task RL literature does not address this mismatch.

### Gaps
- None of these papers report diagnostics for per-head value correlation, collapse, or the quality of dQ/da.
- MultiCriticAL: I could not verify whether its critics are separate networks or separate heads, and did not read its quantitative tables. A search-engine summary says the styles share the same system dynamics; I did not confirm that wording on the page.
- For CARE, I only partly verified how the critic uses the mixture encoder. In the mtrl-based agents I read, the critic is called with `detach_encoder=False` and the actor with `detach_encoder=True`, so the shared encoder is trained by the critic loss only [C, FAMO repo copies of the agent].

## 3. Loss balancing that keeps error-size information (GradNorm, uncertainty weighting, DWA, FAMO, IMTL, Nash-MTL, PopArt): evidence for or against inside a TD-learned critic

### Takeaway
Most of these methods discard absolute error size by design. Uncertainty weighting settles at weights proportional to 1/L_k, so its gradient is proportional to grad log L_k. FAMO and IMTL-L are log-loss or comparable-scale methods, Nash-MTL "is invariant to changes in loss scale", and GradNorm equalises gradient norms (adjusted for relative training rate). That is the same family as our size-blind `--critic-norm`. Only static weights, DWA (bounded weights on the raw losses), PopArt (error relative to the target scale) and categorical cross-entropy critics (bounded gradients, but the loss still depends on fit) keep some error-size information. Several were run inside a SAC critic on Meta-World MT10: uncertainty weighting 0.77, FAMO 0.83, CAGrad 0.83, Nash-MTL 0.91 (0.76-0.80 when re-run by the FAMO authors), against 0.49 for an untuned shared baseline. But a regularised, reward-normalised plain-sum baseline matches them (Kurin et al.), and IMTL crashed in RL. I found no study of any of these inside a decomposed critic.

### Cited Findings
- **GradNorm** (Chen, Badrinarayanan, Lee, Rabinovich; ICML 2018). It learns loss weights so that each task's gradient norm on a chosen layer W ("generally chosen as the last shared layer") moves towards G_bar_W(t) x [r_i(t)]^alpha, where r_i is the relative inverse training rate. It "matches or surpasses the performance of exhaustive grid search methods, despite only involving a single asymmetry hyperparameter alpha". [S] — [arXiv 1711.02257](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02257)
- **Uncertainty weighting** (Kendall, Gal, Cipolla; CVPR 2018). Loss = 1/(2 sigma_1^2) L_1 + 1/(2 sigma_2^2) L_2 + log(sigma_1 sigma_2) (their eq. 7), read as "learning the relative weight of the losses ... adaptively". [S] — [arXiv 1705.07115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07115)
- **DWA** (Liu, Johns, Davison; CVPR 2019). lambda_k(t) = K exp(w_k(t-1)/T) / sum_i exp(w_i(t-1)/T), with w_k = L_k(t-1)/L_k(t-2). It "only requires the numerical task loss". [S] — [arXiv 1803.10704](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10704)
- **FAMO** (Liu, Feng, Stone, Liu; NeurIPS 2023): "decreases task losses in a balanced way using O(1) space and time". The same paper's "SI" baseline "minimizes sum_k log L_k(theta), as SI is invariant to any scalar multiplication of task losses". [S] — [arXiv 2306.03792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03792). In the released MT10 code, the critic is trained on sum_i w_i log(L_i) times a detached constant c, and the weights w are updated from the per-task drop in log loss. [C] — [FAMO repo, mtrl_files/famo.py](https://github.com/Cranial-XIX/FAMO)
- **IMTL** (Liu et al.; ICLR 2021). For shared parameters, it sets closed-form scaling factors so that the aggregated gradient has equal projections onto every task. It also weights task losses so that all stay at a comparable scale, and the authors call the method scale-invariant. [A] — [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2021/poster/2894). In RL: "IMTL was unstable on this RL benchmark and all of the runs crashed due to numerical overflow." [S] — [Kurin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04122)
- **Nash-MTL** (Navon, Shamsian, Achituve, Maron, Kawaguchi, et al.; ICML 2022): "Nash-MTL, is invariant to changes in loss scale". MT10 table: STL 0.90, MTL SAC 0.49, multi-head SAC 0.61, Soft-Mod 0.73, CARE 0.84, PCGrad 0.72, CAGrad 0.83, Nash-MTL 0.91. [S] — [arXiv 2202.01017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01017)
- **PopArt** (van Hasselt, Guez, Hessel, Mnih, Silver; NeurIPS 2016). Adaptive target normalisation that preserves the outputs [A] — [arXiv 1602.07714](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07714). Multi-task form: normalised per-head targets and normalised policy advantages, plus a per-head output-preserving rescale [S] — [Hessel et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04474)
- **Applied inside the critic.**
  - PCGrad: "For actor-critic algorithms ... we simply replace task gradients for both the actor and the critic by their gradients computed via PCGrad." The paper also learns the SAC temperature per task. [S] — [Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2001.06782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06782)
  - Uncertainty weighting inside the SAC critic: per-task `critic_logsigma`, with loss sum 0.5 (exp(-s_k) L_k + s_k), and a separate set for the actor. [C] — [FAMO repo, mtrl_files/uw.py](https://github.com/Cranial-XIX/FAMO)
  - CAGrad's Meta-World agent overrides the gradient hook: it computes per-task gradients over the given parameter list and replaces them with the CAGrad direction. I did not open the base class that calls it for the actor and the critic. [C] — [CAGrad repo, mtrl/mtrl_files/cagrad.py](https://github.com/Cranial-XIX/CAGrad). Results: MT10 0.83 / MT50 0.52, against multi-head SAC 0.61 / 0.45 and one SAC per task 0.90 / 0.74. [S] — [Liu et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2110.14048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14048)
  - FAMO's MT10 table: LS (plain sum, lower bound) 0.49, STL 0.90, PCGrad 0.72, Soft-Mod 0.73, CAGrad 0.83, Nash-MTL as reported 0.91 / 0.85 / 0.87 (update every 1 / 50 / 100 steps), Nash-MTL re-run by the FAMO authors 0.80 / 0.76 / 0.80, uncertainty weighting 0.77, FAMO 0.83. [S] — [arXiv 2306.03792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03792)
  - Soft Modularization weights each task's Q-loss with a softmax over negative per-task temperatures. [S] — [arXiv 2003.13661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13661)
  - PCGrad's effect in RL as Kurin et al. explain it: "the stochastic loss rescaling performed by PCGrad ... reduces the differences in task return scales, and expect that methods like PopArt would have a similar effect without requiring access to per-task gradients". [S] — [arXiv 2201.04122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04122)
  - Categorical critics: "Although reward normalization partially mitigates this issue, we find that adopting a cross-entropy loss via categorical Q-learning effectively balances gradients and ensures consistent learning signals across all tasks" [S] — [BRC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23150). HL-Gauss cross-entropy value training gave "1.8 − 2.1× performance in multi-task setups on Atari" (Farebrother et al.; ICML 2024, venue from memory) [S, intro only] — [arXiv 2403.03950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03950)
  - Gradient-similarity gating in RL: use "the cosine similarity between the gradients of the different tasks" to detect when an auxiliary loss helps the main loss, and down-weight it when it does not (Du, Czarnecki, Jayakumar, Farajtabar, Pascanu, et al.; 2018) [S] — [arXiv 1812.02224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02224)
  - PaCo's loss maskout drops a task with an exploding loss from the shared update. [S] — [arXiv 2210.11653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11653)

### Inferences
- **Uncertainty weighting is the same as our `--critic-norm` at equilibrium.** Minimising Kendall's loss over sigma_k gives sigma_k^2 = L_k (in the code form 0.5 (e^-s L + s), s* = log L_k). The weight becomes 1/(2 L_k) and the parameter gradient becomes 1/2 grad log L_k. That is exactly the scale-invariant log-loss (FAMO's "SI"), and FAMO itself weights grad log L_k. It also behaves like `--critic-norm` (TD error divided by its running RMS, so every loss is about 1). Expected result: the same size-blind failure we measured (21-53% crashes). IMTL-L and Nash-MTL are scale-invariant by construction, so the same applies.
- **GradNorm** equalises the shared-layer gradient norms (scaled by training-rate ratios). With our 100-3000x ratio it would shrink safety's gradient by about that factor. That is the mechanism our scale-invariant aggregators (SVD-PGA, OS-NMF-PGA) already showed to be harmful. I give it low priority.
- **DWA is the exception.** Its weights lie in (0, K) = (0, 4) and multiply the raw losses, so safety's real-unit dominance survives and DWA only shifts emphasis by up to 4x according to loss-descent rates. It is the only listed adaptive method that keeps error size. I found no RL evidence for it, and TD losses with moving targets would make the loss-ratio signal noisy (smoothing would be needed).
- **PopArt is the only one with multi-head shared-critic RL evidence.** Hessel et al. normalise the policy advantages too, which gives equal task weighting in the actor. Our actor must use real units, so a PopArt-normalised trunk allocates features by normalised loss while the actor depends mostly on the real-unit safety slope. That mismatch is consistent with our `--critic-norm-by target` result (collapse fixed, R2 0.97-0.99, crashes 18-56%) and with H1. The prompt does not say whether our version included the output-preserving "Art" step (Hessel eq. 13). Dividing TD errors by a running target std is closer to "Pop" without "Art". This should be checked, but I do not expect it to change the H1 conflict.
- **Most balancing options that keep error size still shift feature share away from safety**, so H1 predicts harm unless safety has its own parameters. These are: static per-group critic-loss weights lambda_k (the Xin/Kurin-endorsed baseline), DWA, and per-head categorical (HL-Gauss) losses with a real-unit support per head. The literature therefore points to balancing only among groups of similar scale, after the architecture isolates safety. Time, precision and efficiency have TD-target spreads of 3.2 / 7.8 / 17 against 152 for safety ([prompt §1](/Users/khushpatel/code/CrazyFlie-2.1/l2f/docs/shared_critic_research_prompt.md)).
- **The MT10 gains of 0.49 to 0.8-0.9 mostly come from fixing reward-scale imbalance between tasks**, which is how Kurin et al. explain the weak plain-sum numbers. In our problem the scale differences are the objective's weighting, not a nuisance. These results should not be read as evidence that balancing helps a decomposed critic.

### Gaps
- No evidence found for GradNorm or DWA inside TD critics.
- None of the multi-task RL papers separates "balancing in the critic" from "balancing in the actor". They all apply both, so the critic-specific effect is unknown.
- No study applies these methods to a decomposed or vector-valued critic whose heads share transitions and whose sum drives one actor.

## 4. Evidence that multi-task optimisation (MTO) methods do not beat tuned scalarisation (Xin et al. 2022; Kurin et al. 2022): implications for our failed critic aggregators

### Takeaway
Both studies find that specialised MTO methods do not beat a properly tuned weighted or unweighted sum. Xin et al. is supervised only and leaves RL as an open question. Kurin et al. include Meta-World SAC with the MTOs applied to a fully shared actor and critic: plain-sum training with standard stabilisers was on par with all of them, and IMTL crashed. Add Recon (gradient surgery does not reduce conflict; changing what is shared does) and BRC (conflict falls with capacity), and the literature predicts our result. Inside a shared critic, aggregators act as implicit loss reweighting or regularisation. They cannot create per-group capacity, so the levers left are architecture and tuned static weighting.

### Cited Findings
- **Xin, Ghorbani, Gilmer, Garg, Firat (NeurIPS 2022)**: "despite the added design and computational complexity of these algorithms, MTO methods do not yield any performance improvements beyond what is achievable via traditional optimization approaches". "MTO algorithms fail to improve the interference profile beyond what is achievable by simple static weighting of the tasks". Performance "is sensitive to basic optimization parameters such as learning rate and weight-decay", and insufficient tuning of the baselines "can create a false perception of performance improvement". "scalarization baselines are often under-tuned". Scope: "we focused our analysis to supervised learning benchmarks. Whether the same behavior holds for reinforcement learning ... is still an open question." They also examine larger models as an alternative use of the extra compute. [S] — [arXiv 2209.11379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11379); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/580c4ec4738ff61d5862a122cdf139b6-Abstract.html)
- **Kurin, De Palma, et al. (NeurIPS 2022)**: specialised MTOs "can be partly interpreted as forms of regularization". In RL (Meta-World MT10/MT50, SAC, fully shared actor and critic, MTOs on per-task gradients over all network parameters), they stabilised the plain-sum baseline with a larger replay buffer, actor L2 regularisation and per-task reward normalisation at sampling time. With that, "unitary scalarization performs on par with other SMTOs", contrary to earlier reports that PCGrad beats it. On MT50 "none of the SMTOs significantly outperforms unitary scalarization, with PCGrad's average being slightly above". A plain-sum run took about 15 hours against more than a week for PCGrad, MGDA and GradDrop. IMTL crashed from numerical overflow. They attribute the low plain-sum numbers in earlier papers to "the lack of reward normalization". [S] — [arXiv 2201.04122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04122)
- **Recon**: gradient surgery "cannot effectively reduce the occurrence of conflicting gradients"; making the high-conflict layers task-specific does. [S] — [arXiv 2302.11289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11289)
- **BRC**: the conflict rate falls with parameter count, so gradient surgery "might not be required after a certain model scale". [S] — [arXiv 2505.23150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23150)
- **Our measured critic-aggregator results** (crashes, against 4.5% for the plain shared critic and 0.6% for variant E): UPGrad 35%, Aligned-MTL 64%, SVD-PGA 85-93%, OS-NMF-PGA 64-95%, PCGrad 7.7%, CAGrad 12.3%. The scale-invariant PGA methods give the tiny time gradient the largest weight. — [prompt §4A](/Users/khushpatel/code/CrazyFlie-2.1/l2f/docs/shared_critic_research_prompt.md)

### Inferences
- **Our aggregator ranking matches what the literature predicts.** PCGrad and CAGrad (c = 0.5) stay closest to the average gradient and were the least bad (7.7% and 12.3%). The more an aggregator rescales by inverse gradient size (SVD-PGA, OS-NMF-PGA, then Aligned-MTL and UPGrad), the more it behaves like a drastic static reweighting against safety, and the worse it crashed. Under Xin and Kurin, the best such a method can do is match a tuned static weighting. Under H1, static down-weighting of safety in a shared trunk is itself harmful.
- **The baseline to beat is itself a tuned plain sum.** Variant E is a hand-tuned scalar TD3, i.e. a tuned unitary scalarisation. Kurin and Xin's evidence says that optimiser changes alone will not beat it. Only a change in the interference profile (what is shared) can.
- **Why the split critic plus a PGA actor worked when MTO inside a shared critic failed:** with separate critics, the 4 actor gradients come from truly distinct value functions, so the aggregator has real conflict to arbitrate (82% precision/efficiency opposition, [prompt §2](/Users/khushpatel/code/CrazyFlie-2.1/l2f/docs/shared_critic_research_prompt.md)). Inside a shared critic, an aggregator can only redistribute one shared capacity among groups.
- **Cheapest literature-endorsed check before any new architecture:** tune the shared critic's learning rate and weight decay, and try a static safety loss weight lambda_safe in {1, 0.3, 0.1}. Xin et al. say this is where false MTO gains usually come from. I expect a small effect (H1), but it settles the "under-tuned baseline" objection for a reviewer.

### Gaps
- Neither study covers TD3, deterministic action-gradients, decomposed critics, or critics' dQ/da quality. Kurin's RL evidence is Meta-World plus SAC only.
- I found no direct study of whether MTO in the critic (as opposed to the actor) helps or hurts in RL.

## 5. For our case: which architecture gives each objective its own capacity where conflict happens while still sharing, with parameter counts and a ranking

### Takeaway
The best-supported partly shared design is to branch at the group level where the conflict is. Give the safety group (rare 400-point crash penalty, 100-3000x larger gradient) its own 64-64 network, and share one 64-64 trunk with 3 heads among time, precision and efficiency: **13,188 parameters, 0.50x the split critic**. Optionally add per-head target normalisation or static weights inside that shared trio only. Second choice: a shared-private (PLE/CGC-style) critic, where every group has a small private expert plus a shared trunk (10,180-15,684). Third: a no-sharing narrow or mixed-width split (8,964-13,252), which is the control that shows whether separation alone suffices. Designs where every parameter stays exposed to every group (MMoE, FiLM, task-embedding, LoRA, PaCo-style composition) are unlikely to fix H1/H4 at our scale. This ranking is my extrapolation: no paper tests partial sharing in a decomposed critic.

### Cited Findings
- **Our scales and failure facts.** TD-target spread: time 3.2, precision 7.8, efficiency 17, safety 152. On the shared hidden layers, safety's loss gradient is 100-3000x larger than time's. A 142-unit wide shared critic still collapses (0.75-0.97). Towers: values distinct on seed 70, but efficiency R2 0.005 at 2M, seed 71 crashed 100%, and seeds 72-75 crashed 8.9% against 2.1% for split. Target-std normalisation fixed collapse but crashed 18-56%. — [prompt §1, §4](/Users/khushpatel/code/CrazyFlie-2.1/l2f/docs/shared_critic_research_prompt.md)
- **One oversized task loss dominating shared critic parameters harms the other tasks** (PaCo quote, Section 2). [S] — [arXiv 2210.11653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11653)
- **Reward-scale disparity is a main cause of interference in shared TD learners** (Hessel et al.; BRC; Kurin et al., Sections 2-3). [S] — [arXiv 1809.04474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04474); [arXiv 2505.23150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23150); [arXiv 2201.04122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04122)
- **Make the high-conflict parts task-specific, rather than doing gradient surgery** (Recon), and let the right amount of sharing differ by task (cross-stitch). [S] — [arXiv 2302.11289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11289); [arXiv 1604.03539](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03539)
- **Explicitly separate task-specific and shared experts to escape the seesaw** (PLE). [A] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3383313.3412236)
- **Separate critics per task remove interference for one multi-task actor** (MultiCriticAL). [A] — [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6568)
- **Task-specific structure in the critic matters in multi-task RL** (PaCo: AC-shared 85.4 vs actor-only 73.3), and diversity constraints in critic experts help (MOORE). [S] — [arXiv 2210.11653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11653); [arXiv 2311.11385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11385)
- **The quality of the critic's action-gradient is improved by a state-conditioned hypernetwork** (Keynan et al.). [S] — [arXiv 2106.06842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06842)

### Inferences
Ranked candidates, best first (my judgement; hypotheses H1-H4 are from the prompt; parameter counts are my count, one twin):

1. **Safety-split hybrid ("conflict-guided branching"): 13,188 params (0.50x split).** Safety gets a full 64-64 network, as in the working split critic. Time, precision and efficiency share a 64-64 trunk with 3 heads.
   - *Mechanism:* removes the 100-3000x gradient and the rare-event targets from the shared trunk, while keeping safety's value and slope exactly as accurate as in the split critic.
   - *Addresses:* H1 (fully) and H4 (the largest gap). H3 only partly: the trio can still collapse among themselves, although their spreads are within about 5x of each other.
   - *Evidence:* PaCo, PopArt, BRC, Kurin, Recon, MultiCriticAL (indirect: none of them is decomposed).
   - *Risk:* the efficiency head may still conflict. It stopped fitting in towers, and precision versus efficiency actor-gradients are opposed 82% of the time.
   - *Before building:* measure Recon-style conflict on the existing shared critic. For each layer and each of the 6 group pairs, log the cosine and norm ratio of the loss gradients. That decides which group(s) to branch. If efficiency also conflicts, use separate safety and efficiency networks with a shared time+precision trunk: 19,652 params (no saving over towers).
   - *Screen:* 2 seeds with the OS-NMF-PGA actor. Measure crashes, circle, waypoint, group-value correlation, per-head R2, and the action-slope cosine.
   - *Optional:* within the trio, apply PopArt with output preservation, or static weights. The H1 risk does not apply there because safety is not in the trunk.
2. **Shared-private, PLE/CGC-style: 10,180 params** (shared 64-64 plus a private 16-16 per group, head on the concatenation), **15,684** (private 32-32), or **13,812** (gated CGC: 2 shared + 4 private 32-32 experts with a per-group gate).
   - *Mechanism:* every group has input-level features that only its own loss trains, and these see (s, a), giving a private action-slope basis (H2). The shared trunk is effectively safety's (H1 kept) and the other groups may reuse it.
   - *Evidence:* supervised only (PLE). In multi-task RL, the critics with task-specific parameters win (PaCo, MOORE).
   - *Risk:* heads may lean on the rich shared (safety-shaped) features and collapse (H3); gates can collapse; more hyperparameters. Log head weights or gate usage and the value correlations.
3. **Narrow or mixed-width split, no sharing: 8,964 params (4 x 32-32), 12,484 (4 x 40-40), 13,252 (safety 64-64 + 3 x 32-32).**
   - *Mechanism:* zero interference by construction. It answers the "4 networks, 3.9x params" concern directly, since 4 x 32-32 is only 1.33x the shared critic.
   - *Evidence:* the strongest (our own split results; MultiCriticAL).
   - *Risk:* too little capacity for precision and efficiency.
   - *Use:* run it as a mandatory control. If it matches the 64-wide split, sharing is unnecessary and parameter count is a non-issue.
4. **Cross-stitch with narrow columns, alpha initialised at or near the identity: 8,996 params.** It starts as option 3 and learns how much to share (supervised evidence: beat a brute-force split search). *Risk:* learned mixing lets the safety gradient leak into the other columns. One-way mixing (other groups read safety's activations but do not train them) would prevent that, but is ad hoc.
5. **MOORE-style orthogonal experts in the critic: 8,980 params** (4 experts of 32-32, a mixing vector per group, a head per group). It targets representation collapse directly and has the strongest multi-task RL record among MoE variants. *Risk:* every expert is still trained by the safety-dominated loss; Gram-Schmidt per sample adds compute; the effect on dQ/da smoothness is unknown.
6. **PaCo-style parameter composition: 13,066 params (K = 2) or 19,599 (K = 3).** Each group is a full network with parameters Phi w_k, and composing the critic mattered in multi-task RL. *Risk:* the shared basis Phi is updated mostly by safety, the composition vectors w_k can converge to each other (the bootstrap loop, H3), and PaCo itself needed loss maskout for dominant losses.
7. **MMoE: 9,540-14,244 params.** All experts are shared, so it does not address H1/H4. A naive MoE baseline was weak in multi-task RL (Soft-Mod's Mix-Expert 42.8% vs 85.0% for multi-head SAC).
8. **LoRA or adapters on a shared trunk: 10,356 (rank 4) or 13,988 (rank 8).** No RL evidence; the shared base is still dominated by safety.
9. **FiLM or task-embedding scalar critic: 6,785-7,553 params, 4 forward passes.** BRC's evidence holds at 4M+ parameters with a cross-entropy loss. At our scale all weights are shared, so I expect it to collapse like the shared critic. It could be used inside option 1's trio.
10. **Hypernetwork critic (state generates action-pathway weights).** Aimed at H2, not at saving parameters (probably larger than split). It is best tested later as an H2 upgrade of the split or hybrid critic.
11. **Soft parameter sharing (4 x 64-64 plus a distance penalty): 26,116 params.** No saving; regularisation only.

Pairing with loss balancing: pair options 1 and 2 with real-unit actor gradients. Inside any shared part that contains safety, keep the plain sum. Inside a shared part that excludes safety, PopArt with output preservation, static weights, or DWA are the principled choices. Avoid uncertainty weighting, FAMO, IMTL, GradNorm and Nash-MTL: they are the size-blind family our `--critic-norm` already represents, and IMTL was unstable in RL. Per-head categorical (HL-Gauss) critics are a principled, evidence-backed scale-robust loss (BRC, Farebrother et al.), but I would test them only after the architecture isolates safety.

Possible cause of the towers instability (untested hypothesis): the critic uses a rational tanh clipped at +-3 (FAST_TANH). Large safety gradients may push shared first-layer pre-activations into saturation, so the private second layers of other groups (efficiency) receive inputs with little information. This would explain efficiency R2 0.005 at 2M. Test: log, per seed, the fraction of layer-1 units saturated. Options 1-3 avoid the issue because each group has its own input-level layer.

### Gaps
- No paper tests partial sharing in a decomposed or reward-component critic, so this ranking extrapolates from multi-task RL (separate MDPs) and supervised MTL.
- Unknown for our critic: the per-layer conflict profile, and the pairwise gradient ratios among time, precision and efficiency. The prompt gives only safety vs time (100-3000x).
- Whether 32-unit per-group networks are enough for precision and efficiency is untested.
- No evidence was found on how gated or orthogonalised architectures affect the smoothness and accuracy of dQ/da in TD3.
