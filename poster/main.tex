    % ======================= LEFT COLUMN =======================
    \begin{column}{.48\textwidth}

    \begin{block}{1. Introduction}
        PID controllers are hand-tuned per drone and must be re-tuned whenever the drone changes.
        We instead train a neural network with reinforcement learning (trial and error with rewards) entirely in simulation,
        and fly it on a real Crazyflie 2.1 Brushless with \textbf{no PID in the loop}.
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{2. The Platform}
        \textbf{Crazyflie 2.1 Brushless} (41\,g). Two Lighthouse base stations give absolute position; the Flow deck adds speed and height.

        \vspace{0.15cm}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{images/LH_CF21b_diagram.png}\\[2pt]
        {\small Drone and Lighthouse setup}
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{3. How It Flies}
        The network runs \textbf{on the drone's own chip} and drives the motors directly, with no PID controller.

        \vspace{0.15cm}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{images/fig_loop_poster.pdf}
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{4. What the Network Sees}
        Every 10\,ms the network gets \textbf{149 numbers}: what the drone measures now, plus a memory of its own recent commands.

        \vspace{0.15cm}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{images/fig_inputs_poster.pdf}
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{Acknowledgements}
        \small
        Our sincere thanks to Dr.\ Yifeng Li for providing the drones, and to Prof.\ Justin Pentesco, the Department of Earth Sciences, and Prof.\ Paul Zelisko from VPMI for letting us use their space to experiment with the drone. Without them, this would not have been possible.
    \end{block}

    \end{column}

    % ======================= RIGHT COLUMN ======================
    \begin{column}{.48\textwidth}

    \begin{block}{5. Training in Simulation}
        \textbf{TD3} learns by trial and error in a simulator of \textbf{our measured drone}, scoring each step for staying on target and flying smoothly. Random delays, weights and position jumps make it robust. \textbf{Never trained on real flights.}

        \vspace{0.15cm}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{images/fig_training_poster.pdf}
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{6. The Mission}
        Take off $\rightarrow$ climb $\rightarrow$ hover $\rightarrow$ circle $\rightarrow$ return $\rightarrow$ \textbf{land on its own}.
        Flown at 0.6, 0.75, 1.0 and 1.5\,m.

        \vspace{0.15cm}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{images/fig_mission_poster.pdf}
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{7. Real-Flight Results}
        On the same drone, our hover matches a published RL controller [3]; circle error is in the range of RL controllers on the older Crazyflie [2]. It lands within \textbf{2--4\,cm} of the start.

        \vspace{0.15cm}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{images/fig_compare_poster.pdf}
    \end{block}

    \begin{block}{8. Future Work}
        Test the controller on more tasks, and build towards a system that uses \textbf{multi-objective optimization}.
    \end{block}
